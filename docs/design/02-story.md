# Story: *Floodword: One Day Late*

> *"The truth is only as fast as whoever carries it."*

## The pitch in one paragraph

You are a courier in the Delta, a land of rivers, reeds and floodgates where news travels only
as fast as boats and feet. One stormy night your boat is deliberately sunk. You wash up alive on
a sandbar with your satchel still sealed, and you watch the first morning ferry carry the news
of **your death** downriver. In your satchel is a confession about the flood that drowned the
town of Hollowmere a year ago: who caused it, who paid for it, and the name of the courier who
delivered the warning one day too late.

The name is yours.

---

## The world: the Delta

A vast river mouth split into channels, held in place by an ancient system of **floodgates**.
Whoever controls the gates decides which land stays dry and which drowns. Six places matter:

| Place | Character | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **Saltmouth** | Busy port city at the sea, warehouses and money | Salt Guild |
| **Gatehold** | Fortress-town built around the great floodgates | Lockwardens |
| **Reedwater** | Stilt village and floating markets in the marshes | Tidefolk |
| **Crossing** | Neutral market town where all roads meet; home of the Post | The Post |
| **Low Fennick** | Farming town below the river level, protected by the gates | Contested |
| **Hollowmere** | The drowned town. Rooftops above the water and bells still in the towers | No one |

**Why this world works (analysis):** every place is defined by its relationship to *water* and
*information*. That keeps the setting thematically tight, and gives every faction a clear,
understandable reason to want the truth hidden or revealed.

---

## The factions

### The Lockwardens: order

Engineers and soldiers who keep the gates. Their creed: *"Better a controlled loss than an
uncontrolled one."* Respected, feared, exhausted.

### The Tidefolk: the river's people

Boat-dwellers and marsh clans who believe the floods are natural and the gates are a wound in the
river. They read the water like scripture, and they saw the Hollowmere flood coming.

### The Salt Guild: prosperity

Merchant houses of Saltmouth. They fund the gates, own the boats, and quietly own most debts in
the Delta. Their creed: *"Everything floats if the price is right."*

### The Post: the neutral thread

The courier network that binds the Delta together, sworn by an oath: *deliver every letter, unopened,
on time.* You were one of them.

**Analysis: no villain faction.** Each faction did something wrong at Hollowmere for a reason a
player could agree with. That's what makes the final choices hard, and decisions only feel heavy
when every option costs something.

---

## Characters

- **You, the Courier.** The player names you. Pronouns are chosen by the player (default: they).
Quick, observant, carrying a secret: a year ago, you took a bribe to deliver one letter a day late.
The go-between never said who was paying or what the letter said. You only learn what it cost in Act II.
- **Kit.** A 12-year-old Hollowmere survivor who runs errands at Crossing. Kit is the first to
find you alive and becomes your companion and your conscience. Kit lost their mother in the flood
and idolises couriers, especially the one everyone says is dead.
- **Warden-General Aldous Brann.** Leader of the Lockwardens. He opened the gates that drowned
Hollowmere to stop a bigger breach that would have flooded Low Fennick and Saltmouth.
300 died to save 9,000. He has never once slept through the night since.
- **Teodor Vale.** Brann's chief engineer. He wrote the confession because he can't live with
the silence. He's in hiding. He has a copy of the Guild's secret payment ledger, passed to him by a
Guild clerk who couldn't stay silent either. That's how he knows about every payment, including the one made to you.
- **Guildmistress Perrin Sabe.** Head of the Salt Guild. She paid Brann to choose Hollowmere over
the Saltmouth warehouses, and she paid a go-between to have the Tidefolk warning delayed, so that
Hollowmere wouldn't empty early and force the Lockwardens to reconsider. A year later she paid someone
to sink your boat. Charming, pragmatic, and never cruel without a reason.
- **Mother Orla Fen.** Tidefolk elder. She sent the warning letter to Hollowmere, the one
you delivered late. Her granddaughter died in the flood.
- **Postmaster Ione Marsh.** Your mentor. She knows about the late letter and covered it up to
protect the Post's reputation, and you.
- **Tally.** A one-eyed messenger crow who adopts you. A gameplay tool (sends your messages) and
a quiet comic relief.

## Story structure

### Prologue: The Drowning

A storm. Your boat is rammed in the dark. You wake on a sandbar at dawn and watch a ferry pass
with a passenger shouting *"The courier's dead! The courier's dead!"*. This is the tutorial for the
rumour map, as you watch the news of your own death travel away from you.

In the satchel: three letters. Two are ordinary. One carries the seal of the Lockwarden engineers.

> **Decision 1: The Seal.** Break the Post's oath and read it, or keep it sealed and find out
> who it was meant for.

### Act I: Ghost

You reach Crossing, where everyone believes you're dead. **Being dead is a tool**: nobody watches you
and no rumours follow you, but nobody trusts a stranger either. Kit recognises you and keeps your secret.

You learn the letter is a confession by Teodor Vale, addressed *"To be read aloud in every town of
the Delta."* If you opened it in the prologue, you already know what it says. If you kept it sealed, Kit
reads the address off the envelope and Ione recognises Teodor's personal engineer's seal. You know
what it is without knowing what it says, and the oath stays unbroken. You track the people who sank your boat to Saltmouth, and they lead to Perrin Sabe.

> **Decision 2: The Ram.** Sabe offers you a fortune and a new life to hand over the letter.
> Accept the bribe, refuse openly, or pretend to accept and blackmail her.

Act I ends when news that **"the courier is alive"** starts spreading, either because you chose to reveal
yourself or because someone saw you. From now on, your reputation is in play.

### Act II: The Late Letter

You find Teodor Vale hiding in Hollowmere's bell tower, the drowned town's only dry room. He
gives you the full confession: Brann's choice, Sabe's money, the Post's cover-up, and a line
near the end:

> *"The Tidefolk's warning was sent two days before. The Guild's ledger shows a payment to the
> courier who carried it, and it arrived one day late. If it had come on time, Hollowmere would have emptied."*

Now the truth will cost you something too. Kit is with you when you read it.

> **Decision 3: Kit's Question.** Kit asks, *"Who was the courier?"* Tell the truth or lie.

Meanwhile, the factions are making moves. The Lockwardens hunt the confession, the Tidefolk
want it read aloud, the Guild wants it burned, and Ione wants it rewritten to protect the Post.

> **Decision 4: The Confession.** Deliver it as written (with your name), alter it to remove your
> name, burn it, sell it, or spread it only as rumour (no proof, just whispers).

Act II ends with Crossing turning dangerous. Lockwarden patrols are hunting the confession, and
everyone knows the errand-runner who follows you around. You send Kit to Mother Orla in Reedwater,
where the Tidefolk take in Hollowmere's orphans. It's the safest place in the Delta, until the rains come.

### Act III: Flood Season

The rains come early. The river is higher than last year. Brann faces the same choice again:
open the gates and drown Reedwater (the Tidefolk village where you sent Kit to be safe),
or risk a breach that floods Low Fennick and Saltmouth.

> **Decision 5: The Gates.** Whose side are you on? Support the Lockwardens' controlled
> sacrifice, help the Tidefolk break the old dikes and let the river find its own path,
> protect the Guild's port, or stand with the Post: no side, just warn everyone.

**The final race.** Whatever you choose, it comes down to a warning that has to arrive in time,
just like last year. You carry it yourself through storm and night. **Whether each town believes
you depends on the reputation you've built all game.** A town that heard you're a liar won't
evacuate. A town that heard you're a hero will run when you shout.

> **Decision 6: The Order.** You can't reach every town in time: three at most, or four if Ione
> lends you the Post's fastest boat (she only does if you kept the seal in the prologue). Choose your route.

This is the game's thesis turned into gameplay: the whole story led to a moment where the speed
and credibility of information decides who lives.

### Epilogue

Each town's fate is shown through the rumours told about you years later. The game ends
with a rumour, not a cutscene, and the final line changes with your ending.

## Why this story works (analysis)

| Craft principle | How the story does it |
|---|---|
| **Theme matches mechanics** | The story is about late information; the gameplay is about information speed. Act III merges them. |
| **Personal stakes and world stakes** | World: who controls the floodgates. Personal: your role in Hollowmere and Kit's trust. |
| **A strong opening hook** | Watching news of your own death sail away is the first 2 minutes of gameplay. |
| **A mirrored structure** | The game starts with a late letter and ends with a warning that must not be late. Players feel the circle close. |
| **No pure villain** | Every faction did wrong for understandable reasons, so no choice is free. |
| **An emotional anchor** | Kit turns abstract politics into one child's trust, which is what players will actually remember. |
